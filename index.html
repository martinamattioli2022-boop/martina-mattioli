// research

Where machine learning meets epistemology

Philosophy of science and ML share deep similarities. Machine learning has even been called the “experimental philosophy of science”. My work connects the two fields systematically, with a focus on explanation, causality and induction.

01

Epistemology meets machine learning

A monograph with Pelillo on how induction, causality and explanation shape both the scientific method and ML itself.

Current

  • Epistemology of Machine Learning: Induction, Causality, Explanation — Springer, Cognitive Technologies (forthcoming)
  • Tutorial at ECAI 2024

Next

  • Teaching and community-building: tutorials, summer-school modules and a workshop on philosophy of science and ML, planned at ECAI 2027
02

Scientific explanation

The debate on scientific explanation rarely reaches XAI. I compare the two historically and use philosophical tools to bring the conceptual rigor XAI is often said to lack.

Current

  • Understanding XAI through the Philosopher’s Lens — ECAI 2024
  • The Missing Link: A Case for Philosophy-Informed XAI — in preparation

Next

  • From principles to evaluation: turning five principles (object of explanation, question-relativity, understanding, causal vs. associational information, truth/approximation) into testable criteria and checklists
  • From explaining the model to explaining the world: XAI for scientific inference and hypothesis generation
  • A reporting template stating what a method explains, what it distorts, and whether its information is causal
03

The black box

Born in WWII and adopted by cyberneticists, the black-box metaphor is richer than it looks. Systems fall into different kinds of black box depending on determinacy and observability.

Current

  • The Black Box Metaphor in Machine Learning and Pattern Recognition: A Philosophical Perspective — under review

Next

  • Explanatory depth: a measure of how deep an explanation is, distinct from how opaque a system is, drawing on Bunge and interventionist accounts
  • Guidelines for regulators, hospitals and developers on what kind of “box” a system is for a given stakeholder
04

Medical explanation

In medicine, patients are in a vulnerable epistemic position. I draw on the philosophy of science and medicine, on causality, trust and epistemic adequacy, to derive principles for medical XAI, and I am conducting a systematic review of how concepts like trust, understanding and accountability are defined and operationalized.

Current

  • Scientific Explanations in Health Sciences: Causality, Trust, and Epistemic Adequacy — ExplImed 2026, IJCAI-ECAI
  • The Concept of Explainability in Medicine — philosophical review, in preparation

Next

  • Empirical studies with clinicians that separate trust, understanding and satisfaction
  • Pluralistic causality: “web of causation” views, including traditional Chinese medicine, informing computational causal models
  • Design principles and an evaluation protocol for medical XAI

Why interdisciplinary

XAI lacks a unified conceptual foundation, while philosophy has debated what makes an explanation adequate for over a century. Philosophers can clarify what a concept requires, ML researchers can say what a method provides, and clinicians can say what a decision needs. Only together can we build explanations that are technically sound, epistemically justified and clinically useful.
